What this actually does (read before you rely on it)
This fetches each page's HTML and reads its og:title, og:description, og:image, and twitter:* meta tags, applying the same fallback logic real platforms use (missing Open Graph tags fall back to the regular page title/description; missing Twitter Card tags fall back to Open Graph). It then renders a close visual approximation of each platform's card. Actual rendering can vary slightly by platform and can be affected by caching — Facebook and LinkedIn especially cache old previews aggressively, so a page you just fixed may still show the old version there until their cache is manually refreshed via their own debugging tools.

How to fix a broken preview

Add Open Graph tags

At minimum, add og:title, og:description, and og:image to your page's <head>. This covers Facebook, LinkedIn, and is also the fallback source for Twitter/X.

Use a proper image size

Set og:image to at least 1200×630px. Smaller or oddly-cropped images often render as a tiny thumbnail instead of a full card.

Set twitter:card explicitly

Add <meta name="twitter:card" content="summary_large_image"> to guarantee a large image card on Twitter/X rather than a small default thumbnail.

Clear the platform's cache

After fixing tags, use Facebook's Sharing Debugger or LinkedIn's Post Inspector to force them to re-scrape the page — otherwise they may keep showing a stale cached preview.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my link look wrong when shared on Facebook or Twitter?

Social platforms build their preview cards from a page's Open Graph tags (og:title, og:description, og:image) and Twitter Card tags. If those tags are missing, the platform falls back to the page's regular title and meta description, and often shows no image at all — which is usually why a shared link looks blank or generic.

What image size should og:image be?

Facebook and LinkedIn recommend at least 1200×630 pixels for og:image so it renders sharply as a large card rather than a small square thumbnail. Twitter/X uses the same image for its summary_large_image card type.

Does this tool change anything on my site?

No — it only reads the page's existing HTML and renders a preview. To fix a broken preview, you need to add or correct the relevant meta tags in your own page's <head>.
